Scroll from here to see what fixed and sticky actually do.

Both of them are about the scroll. fixed leaves the document entirely: it is placed against the viewport and stays there. sticky stays in normal flow, keeping its space, and only pins itself once the scroll would push it past the threshold you gave it.

One caveat worth knowing before you meet it in a debugging session: a fixed element inside an ancestor with a transform, filter or perspective is positioned against that ancestor instead of the viewport. It stops being fixed to the screen and nobody can see why.

A sticky element also needs somewhere to stick: it can only travel inside its own parent. If its parent is exactly as tall as it is, it will never appear to stick at all, and the declaration looks broken when it is not.
